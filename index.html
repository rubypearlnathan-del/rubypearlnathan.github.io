Interactive Course Demo • K–12 Science

Forces, Motion & Balanced Systems (Grade 8)

Module 1 of 3
Learning Objective: Analyze how balanced and unbalanced forces affect an object's motion using real-world evidence (NGSS MS-PS2-2).

Every object around you experiences multiple forces at once—gravity pulling downward, surfaces pushing upward, and friction resisting motion. When all opposing forces are equal in magnitude, the net force is zero, and the object's motion does not change.

Balanced Forces (Net = 0 N)

A book resting on a lab table or a satellite cruising at constant speed in a straight path.

Unbalanced Forces (Net ≠ 0 N)

A soccer ball accelerating after a kick or a parachute slowing a skydiver's descent.

Click each scenario card below to reveal the underlying force interaction:

Scenario A • Click to Flip

Tug-of-War Rope Held Still

Balanced: Both teams pull with 450 N in opposite directions. Net force = 0 N, so acceleration is zero.

Scenario B • Click to Flip

Braking Bicycle on Wet Asphalt

Unbalanced: Friction acts opposite to forward motion, creating negative acceleration until velocity reaches 0 m/s.

Formative Knowledge Check

A cargo drone hovers completely stationary 20 meters above the ground. Which statement accurately describes the forces acting on it?

60-Second Microlearning • Interactive Block Showcase

Spotting Phishing Red Flags in 60 Seconds

Step 1 of 3
Micro-Objective: Identify domain spoofing and artificial urgency before clicking external links.

Modern phishing attacks bypass spam filters by mimicking routine internal notifications (payroll updates, shared documents, or password resets). Use the S.L.U. rule before acting:

1. Sender Domain

Check characters after the @ symbol for lookalike typos (e.g., @micros0ft-support.com).

2. Link Destination

Hover over buttons without clicking to inspect the true destination URL.

3. Urgency Trigger

Be skeptical of 2-hour deadlines threatening account suspension.
